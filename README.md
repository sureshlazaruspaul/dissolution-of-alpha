## Abstract

This paper develops a theoretical model to determine whether capital markets reach Eugene Fama's (1970) Strong Form efficiency in an environment of ubiquitous artificial intelligence. We argue that the integration of AI into corporate and financial decision-making facilitates a transition toward a future state characterized by computational symmetry and the terminal collapse of private information sets.

We analytically characterize a market equilibrium where the adoption of high-capacity, standardized AI models by both insiders and external participants eliminates the interpretive divergence and informational lags that Fama originally categorized as insurmountable frictions. Our model demonstrates that as decision-making and synthesis become derivatives of shared training datasets, traditional informational arbitrage is rendered obsolete. This "algorithmic equilibrium" fundamentally shifts the market's role from a venue of active price discovery to a global capital utility. Our results suggest that under conditions of perfect predictive transparency, idiosyncratic risk is fully internalized and the capacity to generate alpha is structurally neutralized. Consequently, we conclude that optimal investor strategy in such a regime converges toward total indexation—treating market returns as a proxy for global GDP growth, akin to a risk-adjusted savings instrument. We derive the theoretical conditions under which active management ceases to be a viable economic activity and discuss the broader implications for the future of financial intermediation.
